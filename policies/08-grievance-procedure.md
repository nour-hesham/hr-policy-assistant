# Grievance and Complaints Procedure

**Document ID:** HR-POL-008
**Last updated:** February 2025

## Purpose

This procedure provides a fair, consistent and confidential way for employees to raise concerns about their work, working environment or colleagues, and to have those concerns addressed.

The firm encourages employees to raise concerns early, before they escalate.

## Scope

This procedure covers employment-related grievances, including but not limited to:

- Concerns about working conditions or workload;
- Concerns about the behaviour of colleagues or managers;
- Concerns about the application of firm policies to the individual;
- Concerns about pay, benefits or working arrangements (excluding annual compensation reviews, which are governed separately).

Serious concerns about misconduct, fraud or regulatory breaches should be raised under the Code of Conduct's reporting-concerns section, or via the ethics hotline.

## Step 1: Informal resolution

Wherever possible, employees are encouraged to raise concerns informally with their **direct line manager** in the first instance. Many concerns can be resolved through a straightforward conversation.

If the concern is about the line manager themselves, the employee may raise it directly with the manager's manager, or with HR.

## Step 2: Formal written complaint

If the concern cannot be resolved informally, the employee may submit a **formal written complaint to HR**. The complaint must be submitted within **30 calendar days** of the events giving rise to it (or of the informal step, whichever is later).

The complaint should set out:

- A clear description of the concern;
- Relevant dates, people involved, and any supporting information;
- The outcome the employee is seeking.

HR will acknowledge receipt within 3 business days.

## Step 3: Formal review

Upon receipt of a formal written complaint, HR will convene a **review panel within 15 business days**. The panel will normally comprise:

- A senior HR representative;
- A senior manager from an area unrelated to the complaint;
- An additional independent member where appropriate.

The panel may interview the complainant, the person(s) complained about, and any other relevant witnesses. All parties will be given a fair opportunity to be heard.

The panel will issue a written decision within **10 business days** of concluding its review.

## Step 4: Escalation

If the complainant considers the panel's decision to be materially unfair, they may escalate the matter to the **CEO or Board Audit Committee** within 10 business days of the panel's decision. Escalations are considered on written submissions only and are final.

Escalation is not available before Step 3 has been completed.

## Confidentiality

All parties involved in a grievance process are expected to maintain confidentiality. Details of the complaint and the process will be shared only with those who need to know for the purposes of investigation and resolution.

## Protection against retaliation

The firm strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee who raises a grievance in good faith, regardless of whether the grievance is upheld. Any retaliation is itself a serious disciplinary matter.

## Support

Employees using this procedure may be accompanied at any formal meeting by a colleague of their choice. Additional support is available through the firm's Employee Assistance Programme.
