# Work Hours and Overtime Policy

**Document ID:** HR-POL-004
**Last updated:** January 2025

## Standard working hours

Standard working hours are **40 hours per week**, typically worked between 9:00 AM and 5:00 PM, Sunday to Thursday. This includes a one-hour unpaid break for lunch.

## Core hours

All employees, whether working in the office or remotely, are expected to be available between the **core hours of 10:00 AM and 4:00 PM**, when the majority of meetings and collaborative work take place. Time outside these core hours may be flexed by agreement with the line manager, provided the standard weekly hours are met.

## Time recording

Non-exempt employees must record their working hours through the internal time-tracking system on at least a weekly basis. Exempt employees are not required to record hours but must ensure their manager is aware of any material changes to their working pattern.

## Overtime

Overtime is defined as approved work performed in excess of the standard 40 hours per week.

- Overtime must be **pre-approved in writing** by the direct line manager.
- Wherever practical, **time in lieu is preferred over cash payment**. Time in lieu accrues on an hour-for-hour basis and must be taken within 3 months of accrual.
- For **non-exempt roles**, where cash payment is approved:
  - Weekday overtime is paid at **1.5 times** the standard hourly rate.
  - Weekend and public-holiday overtime is paid at **2.0 times** the standard hourly rate.
- **Exempt roles** (typically senior professional and managerial roles) are not eligible for overtime payment. Time-in-lieu arrangements are available in exceptional circumstances at the manager's discretion.

Whether a role is classified as exempt or non-exempt is defined in the employment contract and confirmed by HR.

## Weekend and public-holiday work

Working on weekends or public holidays is not the norm and should be avoided where possible. Where such work is required (for example, for critical deployments or client deadlines), it must be pre-approved by the department head, in addition to the line manager.

## Rest and breaks

Employees are entitled to reasonable rest breaks during the working day and should not routinely work more than 6 consecutive hours without a break. Sustained working patterns exceeding standard hours should be raised with the line manager, who is responsible for addressing workload and resourcing.

## Health and wellbeing

The company does not encourage or reward excessive working hours. Managers are responsible for monitoring the working patterns of their teams and taking appropriate action if patterns suggest workload or resourcing issues.
